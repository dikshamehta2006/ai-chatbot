# Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) Distress Tolerance Skills

## 1. When to Use Distress Tolerance
Distress tolerance skills are designed for crisis moments or intense emotional spikes when you cannot immediately change the situation, but need to avoid making it worse.

## 2. The TIPP Skill (Rapid Nervous System Reset)
Use TIPP when physiological arousal is high (racing heart, panic, intense anger):
- **T - Temperature**: Splash cold water on your face or hold an ice cube in your palm. This triggers the mammalian dive reflex, rapidly downshifting heart rate and activating the parasympathetic nervous system.
- **I - Intense Exercise**: Engage in 1-2 minutes of brief high-intensity physical exertion (jumping jacks, fast stairs, brisk walk) to burn off adrenaline and cortisol.
- **P - Paced Breathing**: Inhale slowly for 4 seconds, exhale slowly for 7 seconds. Extending the exhale stimulates the vagus nerve and signals safety to the body.
- **P - Paired Muscle Relaxation**: Consciously tense a muscle group (like shoulders or fists) tightly on the inhale, then completely release tension on the exhale. Notice the physical sensation of release.

## 3. The STOP Skill
In moments of impulsive reactivity:
- **S - Stop**: Pause immediately. Do not react, speak, or send a message. Freeze in place.
- **T - Take a Step Back**: Remove yourself physically or mentally from the immediate stimulus. Take a deep breath.
- **O - Observe**: Notice what is happening inside you (feelings, racing thoughts) and around you (facts of the situation). Be an objective observer without judgment.
- **P - Proceed Mindfully**: Ask yourself: "What action will genuinely help this situation? What aligns with my values?"

## 4. Radical Acceptance
Pain is an unavoidable part of life; suffering is pain multiplied by resistance.
- Radical acceptance means acknowledging reality as it is in this exact moment, without approving, liking, or resigning to it.
- Affirmation: "This situation is painful, but it is here. Fighting against reality won't change the past; accepting this moment gives me power to decide my next step."
